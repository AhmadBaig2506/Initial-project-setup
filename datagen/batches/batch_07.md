Pasteurization makes milk and many other beverages safer to drink by using controlled heat to destroy disease-causing microorganisms and reduce the number of organisms that cause spoilage. The process can also change a beverage’s flavor, aroma, texture, and nutritional properties, although the effects depend on the temperature, heating time, composition of the drink, and how it is packaged and stored afterward. In milk, pasteurization is designed to eliminate harmful bacteria without producing the pronounced cooked flavor associated with more intensive heat treatments. It does not make milk completely sterile, and it does not prevent all spoilage. Instead, it reduces microbial risks and extends the time milk remains suitable for consumption when handled and refrigerated properly. The same principle applies to many other beverages, including fruit juices, cider, beer, and certain plant-based drinks. However, each beverage presents different challenges because its acidity, sugar content, proteins, and other ingredients influence how microorganisms respond to heat. Keystone species A keystone species is a species that has a disproportionately large effect on its natural environment relative to its abundance. The concept was introduced in 1969 by the zoologist Robert T. Paine. Keystone species play a critical role in maintaining the structure of an ecological community, affecting many other organisms in an ecosystem and helping to determine the types and numbers of various other species in the community. Without keystone species, the ecosystem would be dramatically different or cease to exist altogether. Some keystone species, such as the wolf and lion, are also apex predators. The role that a keystone species plays in its ecosystem is analogous to the role of a keystone in an arch. While the keystone is under the least pressure of any of the stones in an arch, the arch still collapses without it. Similarly, an ecosystem may experience a dramatic shift if a keystone species is removed, even though that species was a small part of the ecosystem by measures of biomass or productivity.
