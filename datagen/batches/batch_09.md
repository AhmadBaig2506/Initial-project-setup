Sedimentary rocks form through a series of processes: weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction, and cementation. First, existing rocks are broken down by weathering, then erosion moves these fragments to new environments. Over time, deposition settles these sediments, layer upon layer. Compaction squeezes these layers, and cementation then binds them, ultimately forming solid rock. The sources of these sediments are varied, ranging from the weathering of pre-existing rocks to the remains of once-living organisms, or even minerals precipitated from water. These rocks typically form in layers (strata), often in environments like riverbeds, lakes, and oceans.
