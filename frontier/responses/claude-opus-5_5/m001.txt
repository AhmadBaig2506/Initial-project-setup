Question: What is the difference between a virus and a bacterium?

A bacterium is a complete living cell (a prokaryote) with its own machinery such as ribosomes, while a virus is a non-cellular particle made of protein, nucleic acid and sometimes a lipid envelope that cannot reproduce without a living host [1][2][3]. Bacteria contain both DNA and RNA and reproduce by binary fission, whereas a virus has DNA or RNA but never both and is assembled inside a host cell [1][3]. Bacteria are also much larger, typically 0.5 to 5 μm compared with 0.02 to 0.2 μm for viruses [1][3]. Antibiotics kill or block the growth of many bacteria but do not treat viruses, because they target bacterial structures like the cell wall and ribosomes that viruses lack, while antiviral medicines help the body clear some viruses [1][3][5]. Bacteria are considered living, whereas whether viruses are alive is debated and they are usually classed as non-living [3].
