Refrigerators work primarily on the principles of thermodynamics, specifically the refrigeration cycle. This cycle involves the evaporation and condensation of a refrigerant, a fluid with a low boiling point. The process starts when the refrigerant evaporates inside the evaporator coils, absorbing heat from the interior of the refrigerator and thereby lowering the temperature of the stored food. This heat absorption occurs as the refrigerant transitions from a liquid to a gas, allowing it to effectively absorb and carry heat away. Once the refrigerant has absorbed enough heat, it is then compressed by a compressor, raising its pressure and temperature. The hot, high-pressure gas then passes through the condenser coils located outside the refrigerator, where it releases the absorbed heat to the environment. As the refrigerant cools down, it condenses back into a liquid form and returns to the evaporator coils to begin the cycle anew.
